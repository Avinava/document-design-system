Summary

The ingestion path holds 41% of the platform footprint behind a single queue with no failover. This proposes splitting it into two independently deployable paths so that a queue failure degrades throughput rather than stopping ingestion. Written last, as a summary should be.

Problem

Four of the last six incidents traced to the same coupling.1 Each began as queue backpressure and ended as a full ingestion stop, because every producer shares one path.

State the evidence, not the feeling. "The current system is hard to maintain" is an opinion; the incident count is a problem.

Goals

  • A queue failure degrades throughput instead of halting ingestion.
  • Recovery without a coordinated redeploy of every producer.
  • No change to the producer-facing API.

Non-goals

This does not propose replacing the queue technology, changing the retention policy, or touching the batch path. Those are real questions and they are not this document's questions.

This is the section most often omitted and most often needed. It keeps the review from sprawling into adjacent problems, and it is where a reviewer with a different mental model discovers the mismatch early — while it is still cheap.

Proposal

Route producers through a thin dispatcher that writes to one of two queues keyed by partition. Each queue has its own consumer group and its own failover. The dispatcher is stateless and deployable independently. 

yamldispatcher:
  routes:
    - match: {partition: even}
      queue: ingest-a
    - match: {partition: odd}
      queue: ingest-b
  on_queue_unavailable: shed_and_alert

Keep lines under about 80 characters. Long lines force horizontal scrolling on screen and clip in print.

Alternatives considered

  • Do nothing. Always an option, and often chosen by default. Cost: the incident pattern continues at roughly one per quarter.
  • Larger single queue. Raises the backpressure threshold without removing the single point of failure. Rejected because it changes when the incident happens, not whether.
  • Per-producer queues. Removes the coupling entirely, but multiplies operational surface by the producer count. Rejected on cost.

An alternatives section listing only strawmen tells the reader the decision was made before the document was written, and they will read the rest accordingly.

Risks and mitigations

  • Partition skew sends most traffic to one queue. Mitigated by hashing on event ID rather than producer ID.
  • Dispatcher becomes the new single point of failure. Mitigated by running it stateless behind the existing load balancer.

Open questions

  1. Does the consumer group rebalance cleanly under partial failure? — Owner: Platform, by 2026-08-25
  2. Is shed-and-alert the right behavior, or should the dispatcher block? — Owner: Reliability, by 2026-08-25

A document with no open questions is either finished or not being honest.
